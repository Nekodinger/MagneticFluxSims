The key ideas

Magnetic flux density, B

The force per unit current per unit length on a straight conductor placed at right angles to the field.

B = F / (IL)

Unit: tesla, T. 1 T = 1 N A−1 m−1 = 1 Wb m−2. On a diagram, B is shown by how closely the field lines are packed. Increase B above and the lines crowd together.

Magnetic flux, Φ

The product of the flux density and the area perpendicular to the field.

Φ = BA cos θ

Unit: weber, Wb (1 Wb = 1 T m2). θ is measured between B and the normal to the loop. Flux is largest when the loop faces the field and zero when the field skims along its plane.

Flux linkage, NΦ

A coil of N turns links the flux N times.

flux linkage = NΦ = NBA cos θ

Unit: Wb (sometimes written Wb-turns). This is the quantity whose rate of change gives the induced e.m.f. in Faraday's law.

Watch the angle

Questions often give the angle α between the field and the plane of the coil. Then θ = 90° − α, so

Φ = BA sin α

Press "Look along B": the loop's outline you see is exactly the perpendicular area A cos θ.

Check yourself

1. A coil of 200 turns and area 5.0 × 10−3 m2 sits with its plane perpendicular to a field of 0.40 T. Find the flux and the flux linkage.

Plane perpendicular to B means the normal is parallel to B, so θ = 0°.

Φ = BA = 0.40 × 5.0 × 10−3 = 2.0 × 10−3 Wb

NΦ = 200 × 2.0 × 10−3 = 0.40 Wb

2. The same coil is turned so its plane makes 30° with the field. Find the new flux.

Angle to the plane α = 30°, so the angle to the normal θ = 60°.

Φ = 0.40 × 5.0 × 10−3 × cos 60° = 1.0 × 10−3 Wb

3. A flux of 3.0 mWb passes at right angles through an area of 0.015 m2. What is the flux density?

B = Φ / A = 3.0 × 10−3 / 0.015 = 0.20 T

4. Through which angles does the flux become zero, and when is it negative?

Zero at θ = 90° and 270°, when the field lines run parallel to the loop's plane. Negative between 90° and 270°: the field now enters through the other face of the loop. Set the slider to 180° to see Φ = −BA.
